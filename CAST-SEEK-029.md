# 0.29.0-alpha / asynchronous cast seek recovery

The old receiver called seekTo without a completion callback, did not distinguish buffering from playback completion, and unconditionally cleared sender play intent on every completion event. Play also left the service completed flag set after a reported end and could not rebuild an errored MediaPlayer.

The receiver now owns explicit Play/Pause intent, queued/current seek targets, buffering, completion and stop state. It listens for seek completion and buffering start/end, reports TRANSITIONING while seeking/buffering, and reapplies the latest user intent when the seek settles. Pending seeks survive preparation, rapid seek changes keep the latest pending target, and sender Seek targets are parsed from the command arguments rather than a shared position field. Progress polling no longer overwrites an outstanding target with the old playback position. Pausing during a seek does not request playback on completion.

A completion during seeking/buffering, a known-duration early EOF or a suspicious end immediately following a seek is treated as an interrupted transition rather than immediately reporting a finished track. One automatic reopen of the same media URI at the intended/current position is allowed per recovery chain. Network/time-out errors during a seek/buffer transition use the same bounded recovery. A seek callback missing for 45 seconds also gets that bounded recovery. Explicit new Seek/Play permits a new attempt; repeated failures stop with an actionable error instead of retrying forever. Play after a failure recreates the player; Play after a genuine end seeks back to the beginning.

All media callbacks are checked against the current player and load generation. Stop clears pending seek work and user play intent; Back still invalidates queued commands, releases media and clears receiver URIs, so late callbacks cannot restart an exited cast. Existing surfaces, photo casts, metadata/artwork views, background receiver controls and decoder choices remain. The audio record UI still derives its spin indicator from actual MediaPlayer.isPlaying; this patch does not add an audio buffer bar or change its artwork rendering.

These checks are recovery heuristics, not proof of the remote server's buffering or range behavior. Unknown-duration true/premature EOF cannot always be distinguished. Sources that report seeking unavailable retain playback instead of receiving a seek. Actual seeking still depends on the sender's HTTP range/time seeking, media format/index and Android decoder support. Reopening may reconnect/download additional stream data and cannot make a nonseekable or expired source seekable. No transcoder, alternate media engine or media download cache is added.

No Android build, device test or sender HTTP trace was run. Verify CI and seek beyond buffered media, rapid scrubbing, pause/resume mid-seek, remote/local seeking, true end/replay, unknown-duration streams, bad range responses, network interruption, recovery bounds, switching media, rotation/surfaces and Back/Stop cancellation on the actual sender and frame.
